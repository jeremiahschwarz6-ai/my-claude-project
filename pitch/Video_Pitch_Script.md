# Video Pitch Script — Splendid Spoon Social Media Strategy
**STCO 348 · Target runtime: 7:30 (safe inside the 5–10 minute window)**

**How to use this:** these are talking points, not a teleprompter. The assignment
explicitly penalizes reading the slides. Learn the *shape* of each beat — the opening
line, the number, the closing line — and let the middle be conversational. Practice
twice with a timer before recording.

**Framing:** you are a contract strategist delivering a final presentation to the
client. Speak to Splendid Spoon as "you," not "they."

---

## [0:00–0:35] Slide 1 — Stop renting your customers

Open on camera, not on the slide.

> "Splendid Spoon doesn't have a marketing problem. It has a landlord problem.
> Right now, almost every new customer you get, you rent — from Meta, by the
> impression, at whatever the auction costs that day. Over the last few weeks I've
> looked at your business, your customer, and all nine of your channels. Today I want
> to show you how to stop renting and start owning. My name is ____, and this is
> your social media strategy."

*Delivery: slow, level, no rush. This is the thesis of the entire pitch.*

---

## [0:35–1:15] Slide 2 — The engine that built you

> "Paid social built this company, and it worked. But here's the structural problem:
> every competitor can outbid you for the same woman, on the same platform, on the
> same afternoon. 71% of U.S. adults are on Facebook, which is exactly why it's
> crowded and expensive."

Point at the zero.

> "And this is the number that concerns me. Zero owned channels compounding discovery
> for you today. Everything you have, you're leasing."

---

## [1:15–1:45] Slide 3 — Who we're working with

Move fast. They know their own company.

> "Quick grounding. Founded 2013 in a rented Brooklyn pizza kitchen by Nicole Centeno —
> a trained chef *and* a published research biologist. That combination matters later.
> Since the Mosaic merger in January you're at $190 million cumulative revenue, 200+
> products, and — the big one — you can now manufacture your own food."

---

## [1:45–2:25] Slide 4 — Two trends

> "Two forces are pulling against each other. Plant-based retail has now declined two
> years running — down 4%, then down 2%, to $7.9 billion. The category tailwind that
> launched you has reversed.
>
> But prepared meal delivery is going from $13.7 to $30.7 billion.
>
> So here's my first recommendation, and it's a positioning one: stop selling the diet.
> Sell the time. Plant-based is *how* the food is made. It is not *why* she buys it."

---

## [2:25–3:05] Slides 5 & 6 — The audience and the insight

> "Who is 'she'? Working mothers, 30 to 45, dual-income metro households above $100K.
> Flexitarian, not vegan. 73.9% of American mothers are now in the labor force, and
> the country is down to 43 minutes a day on food prep and cleanup."

Pause. Then slide 6, slower:

> "Which gives us the insight the whole strategy rests on. **She is not short on
> nutrition knowledge. She is short on minutes.** Everyone else in this category sells
> aspiration. We're going to sell relief."

*Two full beats of silence after "relief." Let it land.*

---

## [3:05–3:45] Slides 7 & 8 — The tilt and the voice

> "Joe Pulizzi calls this the content tilt — the narrow space where nobody's competing.
> Plant-based recipes? Saturated. Weight loss? Crowded, and it's a promise your product
> can't make. Your tilt is narrower and nobody owns it: **the zero-prep weekday meal.**
> Breakfast and lunch, when time is shortest and willpower is lowest.
>
> The through-line is 'one plant-based choice a day.' A habit, not a conversion.
>
> And the voice: the calm, capable friend who already solved the problem you're still
> worrying about. On Aaker's brand personality dimensions that's sincerity and
> competence — deliberately not excitement. You lead with relief. Never guilt."

---

## [3:45–4:45] Slides 9 & 10 — The audit  ⟵ *the turn*

> "So where is her attention? YouTube reaches 84% of U.S. adults. Facebook 71.
> Instagram 50."

Then slide 10. Walk the grid.

> "And here's where you actually are. I graded all nine channels against the research.
> Instagram, your blog, and Facebook are genuinely excellent — A's. Podcasts, a B.
> LinkedIn's a C. TikTok, YouTube, and Reddit are D's.
>
> And then there's this one."

Stop on the F. Let it sit.

> "Pinterest. An F. Not because you're doing it badly — because you aren't there at all."

---

## [4:45–5:45] Slides 11 & 12 — The recommendation and Pinterest

> "So: stop renting attention, start owning discovery. Three moves and one retreat.
>
> Priority one is Pinterest, and the numbers are almost unfair. 93% of Pinterest users
> are there to plan a purchase. 86% use it while grocery shopping. They save one to
> three months ahead — they're planning, not reacting. And food is the single largest
> category on the platform.
>
> A subscription that sells *planned weekly eating* to women who *research purchases in
> advance* — that is a description of the Pinterest user. And here's the mechanic that
> matters: an Instagram post is dead in a day. A pin keeps working for months.
>
> Account live in 60 days, 25 pins a month, 50,000 monthly impressions by month six."

---

## [5:45–6:25] Slides 13 & 14 — YouTube, Reddit, and the retreat

> "Priority two, YouTube — 84% of adults and you have no channel. Food is visual,
> preparation is demonstrable; a two-minute meal is already a two-minute video.
>
> Priority three, Reddit. 77% of people now add the word 'Reddit' to a Google search
> when they want a real recommendation. Claim the account, have Nicole do an AMA, and
> answer the delivery complaints in public instead of deleting them.
>
> And one retreat: TikTok. You've got 3,480 followers — about 3% of your Instagram
> audience — and its heaviest users are the people least able to afford a $13 meal.
> Keep it alive with repurposed Reels, stop funding original production for it.
> A strategy that only adds things is a wish list. Prioritizing means saying what you stop."

---

## [6:25–7:10] Slide 15 — The roadmap

> "First 30 days: claim Reddit, build the Pinterest boards, shift 20% of paid budget to
> win-back. Day 60: Pinterest live. Day 90: YouTube live. Month 12: 10,000 YouTube
> subscribers, 30% more organic sessions, and acquisition cost held flat.
>
> Every one of those numbers is falsifiable. That's deliberate — you can fire me on the
> metrics."

*Small smile. It signals confidence.*

---

## [7:10–7:30] Slide 16 — Close

> "You've built a genuinely good product and a brand people trust. The only thing
> missing is ownership of the audience you've been renting. Pinterest in 60 days,
> YouTube in 90, one plant-based choice a day — and the lunch hour belongs to you
> before somebody else takes it.
>
> Thank you."

Hold eye contact for two seconds before stopping the recording.

---

## Delivery checklist

| | |
|---|---|
| **Timing** | Rehearse twice with a stopwatch. If you run long, compress slide 3 and slide 8 — they carry the least argument. |
| **Energy** | Lift your volume about 20% above conversation. Enthusiasm reads as flat on camera. |
| **Eyes** | Look at the lens, not your own video preview. |
| **Framing** | Head and shoulders, eyes a third down from the top. Light in front of you, window behind the camera, not behind you. |
| **Pacing** | Three deliberate pauses: after "short on minutes," after "sell relief," and on the Pinterest F. |
| **Don't** | Read the slides. The deck is evidence behind you; you are the argument. |
| **Audio** | Record somewhere soft — a room with curtains or carpet. Phone earbuds beat a laptop mic. |
| **Backup** | Do one full take without stopping, even if you fumble. A complete imperfect take is insurance. |
