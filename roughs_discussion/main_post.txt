Roughs Critique – Shood Campaign Poster

Hi everyone! My campaign is for Shood, a shoe brand. The goal of my poster is to get people, especially teens and young adults who are hard on their shoes, to replace their worn-out pairs with fresh new ones from Shood. Here are my three roughs.

Rough 1: Old vs. New. This layout splits the poster down the middle, with a worn-out shoe on the left and a brand-new Shood shoe on the right. The headline "SHOOD" and the tagline "Out with the old, step into new" sit in the bottom third. I chose this because a before-and-after comparison tells the story quickly, even from far away. The design principle I focused on was contrast, since the clean new shoe should feel like the obvious winner.

Rough 2: The Hero Shoe. This one shows one large shoe as the main focal point, with the Shood name on the side of the shoe. I used heavy shading to create strong value contrast so the shoe stands out against the white background. The text and logo are smaller and lower on the page to create a clear hierarchy: shoe first, then the message.

Rough 3: Footprints. A path of footprints starts large in the bottom left corner and gets smaller as it moves up toward the "SHOOD" headline in the top right. The footprints create movement and lead the viewer's eye directly to the brand name. I left a lot of negative space so the path is the only thing competing for attention, and the idea connects to the message that every step counts.

I think Rough 2 has the strongest visual impact, but Rough 1 communicates the strategy most clearly. In my next round, I plan to replace the swoosh-style mark with an original Shood logo, add more wear and tear to the old shoe, and write final taglines for Roughs 2 and 3.

I'd love feedback on these questions:
1. Which rough grabs your attention the most, and why?
2. Does the message of the campaign come across without explanation?
3. Does anything feel unbalanced or empty?

Thanks for looking!
